Who Steps In? The Philosophy Behind Fixing Your Broken Sales System

Most companies have a CRM. Very few have someone who truly steps in to own it[cite: 1].

Leads fall through the cracks[cite: 1]. Reps build workarounds[cite: 1]. Data goes missing[cite: 1]. Deals slow down for reasons nobody can explain[cite: 1]. The friction is there—you just can't see it yet[cite: 1].

After years in sales at Yelp, Constant Contact, and multiple startups—and building HubSpot and Close systems for B2B sales teams—I developed a philosophy to make that friction visible[cite: 1]. At Seamless Outbound, we call it WHO STEPS IN[cite: 1].

It is both a question and an audit[cite: 1]. Ten letters representing ten blind spots that most CRMs never expose[cite: 1].

The W-H-O S-T-E-P-S I-N Framework

W

Workarounds: What are reps doing *instead* of your system?

Shadow spreadsheets. Personal texts to leads. Notes in Apple Notes instead of the CRM[cite: 1]. Every workaround is a confession—the system failed the person using it[cite: 1]. Mapping what people avoid tells you exactly where the real friction lives[cite: 1].

H

Human Error: What can a new employee get wrong on day one?

If it can be screwed up, it will be[cite: 1]. A system should be so clear that a smart person with zero context cannot fail it[cite: 1]. Walk through your CRM as if it's your first day[cite: 1]. Where do you hesitate? Where do you guess? Those are design failures, not people failures[cite: 1].

O

Obfuscation: What breaks when your volume doubles?

Most friction doesn't announce itself[cite: 1]. It hides inside layers of workarounds, extra fields, and redundant stages[cite: 1]. What looks fine at 20 deals a month collapses at 200[cite: 1]. We find the breaking point before it costs you money[cite: 1].

S

Sucks & Super: Interview your best and lost customers

Run deep Jobs to Be Done interviews[cite: 1]. Ask your best customers what they were doing right before they found you[cite: 1]. Then do the same with people who left[cite: 1]. Always end with one final question: "How did you feel during the sales and onboarding process?"[cite: 1] That single answer tells you more than everything else combined[cite: 1].

T

Time Per Stage: Where are deals sitting and dying silently?

Time gaps between stages are silent deal killers[cite: 1]. Nobody logs them, but they quietly eat your pipeline[cite: 1]. Measure the actual time between milestones—from form fill to first human contact, and from first call to follow-up[cite: 1]. Speed to lead changes everything[cite: 1].

E

Enrichment: Are ideal customers actually filling your pipeline?

If they aren't, don't pretend that's not a problem[cite: 1]. Have someone step in to own the answer—with a name and a due date[cite: 1]. If the wrong leads are flooding your pipeline, no amount of CRM optimization will save your close rate[cite: 1].

P

Purpose Filter: Does this tool, step, or field actually serve growth?

Every field, workflow, stage, and dashboard must earn its place by serving one of two purposes: get more customers or keep existing customers[cite: 1]. If it doesn't do one of those two things, cut it[cite: 1]. Marie Kondo your entire CRM[cite: 1].

S

Step Backwards: Walk a closed deal in reverse

Pick a closed customer and walk the pipeline backward[cite: 1]. At each stage ask: is this step truly necessary, and what would have made the previous step easier?[cite: 1] Simplicity only reveals itself in reverse[cite: 1].

I

Intelligence: Have you mystery-shopped your own sales process?

Most companies have never actually been their own customer[cite: 1]. Have a trusted contact enter your system exactly as a cold prospect would[cite: 1]. Document how it feels at every single touchpoint[cite: 1]. You cannot fix what you have never personally experienced[cite: 1].

N

No Blind Spots: What does your team hate but never say out loud?

Your reps and ops people know exactly what's broken[cite: 1], but they stopped saying it out loud[cite: 1]. Create a safe way to ask what they love, what they hate, and what hasn't been used in 90 days[cite: 1]. A bloated system is not a powerful system; it's a confusing one[cite: 1].

The Bottom Line

Most RevOps audits look at the system[cite: 1]. WHO STEPS IN looks at what the system is hiding[cite: 1].

If nobody in your company can answer “who steps in to own and simplify the CRM?”—that's the answer[cite: 1]. That's the friction[cite: 1].
