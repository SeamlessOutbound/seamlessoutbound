Who Steps In? The Philosophy Behind Fixing Your Broken Sales System

Most companies have a CRM. Very few have someone who truly steps in to own it.

Leads fall through the cracks. Reps build workarounds. Data goes missing. Deals slow down for reasons nobody can explain. The friction is there—you just can't see it yet.

After years in sales at Yelp, Constant Contact, and multiple startups—and building HubSpot and Close systems for B2B sales teams—I developed a philosophy to make that friction visible. At Seamless Outbound, we call it WHO STEPS IN.

It is both a question and an audit. Ten letters representing ten blind spots that most CRMs never expose.

The W-H-O S-T-E-P-S I-N Framework

W

Workarounds: What are reps doing *instead* of your system?

Shadow spreadsheets. Personal texts to leads. Notes in Apple Notes instead of the CRM. Every workaround is a confession—the system failed the person using it. Mapping what people avoid tells you exactly where the real friction lives.

H

Human Error: What can a new employee get wrong on day one?

If it can be screwed up, it will be. A system should be so clear that a smart person with zero context cannot fail it. Walk through your CRM as if it's your first day. Where do you hesitate? Where do you guess? Those are design failures, not people failures.

O

Obfuscation: What breaks when your volume doubles?

Most friction doesn't announce itself. It hides inside layers of workarounds, extra fields, and redundant stages. What looks fine at 20 deals a month collapses at 200. We find the breaking point before it costs you money.

S

Sucks & Super: Interview your best and lost customers

Run deep Jobs to Be Done interviews. Ask your best customers what they were doing right before they found you. Then do the same with people who left. Always end with one final question: "How did you feel during the sales and onboarding process?" That single answer tells you more than everything else combined.

T

Time Per Stage: Where are deals sitting and dying silently?

Time gaps between stages are silent deal killers. Nobody logs them, but they quietly eat your pipeline. Measure the actual time between milestones—from form fill to first human contact, and from first call to follow-up. Speed to lead changes everything.

E

Enrichment: Are ideal customers actually filling your pipeline?

If they aren't, don't pretend that's not a problem. Have someone step in to own the answer—with a name and a due date. If the wrong leads are flooding your pipeline, no amount of CRM optimization will save your close rate.

P

Purpose Filter: Does this tool, step, or field actually serve growth?

Every field, workflow, stage, and dashboard must earn its place by serving one of two purposes: get more customers or keep existing customers. If it doesn't do one of those two things, cut it. Marie Kondo your entire CRM.

S

Step Backwards: Walk a closed deal in reverse

Pick a closed customer and walk the pipeline backward. At each stage ask: is this step truly necessary, and what would have made the previous step easier? Simplicity only reveals itself in reverse.

I

Intelligence: Have you mystery-shopped your own sales process?

Most companies have never actually been their own customer. Have a trusted contact enter your system exactly as a cold prospect would. Document how it feels at every single touchpoint. You cannot fix what you have never personally experienced.

N

No Blind Spots: What does your team hate but never say out loud?

Your reps and ops people know exactly what's broken, but they stopped saying it out loud. Create a safe way to ask what they love, what they hate, and what hasn't been used in 90 days. A bloated system is not a powerful system; it's a confusing one.

The Bottom Line

Most RevOps audits look at the system. WHO STEPS IN looks at what the system is hiding.

If nobody in your company can answer “who steps in to own and simplify the CRM?”—that's the answer. That's the friction.
